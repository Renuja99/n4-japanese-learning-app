Grammar points on page 34

1

～ながら…

while doing ~

  • Form: verb ます-stem (drop ます) + ながら
  • Used when you do the main action (…) and, at the same time, a second "side" action (~). The verb before ながら must be an action that continues for a while.
  • Examples from the book:
    1. きれいな海うみを見みながらさんぽしました。 I took a walk while looking at the beautiful sea.
    2. 母ははは音楽おんがくを聞ききながら料理りょうりを作つくります。 My mother cooks while listening to music.
    3. アルバイトをしながら大学だいがくに通かよいました。 I went to university while working a part-time job.
2

～ところです

be just about to / be in the middle of / have just done

  • Form: verb dictionary form / ている form / た form + ところです
  • Shows which stage of an action you are at:
    • Dictionary form + ところ = just before the action starts
    • ている + ところ = in the middle of the action
    • た form + ところ = just after the action finished
  • Examples from the book:
    1. あ、試合しあいが始はじまるところですよ。早はやく、早はやく。 Oh, the match is just about to start! Hurry, hurry!
    2. 今いま、インターネットで店みせの場所ばしょをしらべているところです。 I'm looking up the shop's location on the internet right now.
    3. サラ「もしもし、今いまどこ？もう駅えきにいるの？」 トム「うん、ちょうど今こん駅えきに着ついたところだよ。」 Sarah: "Hello, where are you? Are you at the station already?" Tom: "Yeah, I've just this moment arrived at the station."
3

～まで… / ～までに…

until ~ / by ~

  • Form: noun / verb dictionary form + まで・までに
  • まで = "until". The action after it continues the whole time up to that point.
  • までに = "by (a deadline)". The action after it happens once, at some point before that deadline.
  • Examples from the book:
    1. ひこうきの出発時間しゅっぱつじかんまでここで待まっています。 I'll wait here until the plane's departure time.
    2. この仕事しごとが終おわるまで帰かえらないでください。 Please don't go home until this work is finished.
    3. 二十日はつかまでに旅行りょこうのお金かねをはらいます。 I'll pay for the trip by the 20th.
    4. おきゃくさんが来くるまでにへやをかたづけてね。 Tidy the room before (by the time) the guests come, OK?

Practice 16 questions
Instructions

　の中なかの言葉ことばを正ただしい形かたちにして、書かいてください。

Change the word in the brackets ( ) into the correct form and write it in the blank.

Q1 Write

兄あには「うん、うん」と　ながら、わたしの話はなしを聞ききました。（言いう）

My older brother listened to what I was saying while saying "uh-huh, uh-huh."

Show answerHide answer
Answer言いい（言いいながら）

Grammarます-stem + ながら

ながら attaches to the ます-stem. 言いいます → drop ます → 言いい. So 言いいながら = "while saying." The main action is 聞ききました (listened); saying "uh-huh" is the side action happening at the same time.

Ani wa "un, un" to iinagara, watashi no hanashi o kikimashita.

Q2 Write

A「あれ？まだ仕事しごとがあるの？」
B「いえ、今いま　ところです。」（帰かえる）

A: "Huh? Do you still have work to do?"
B: "No, I'm just about to go home now."

Show answerHide answer
Answer帰かえる（帰かえるところです）

GrammarDictionary form + ところです (just about to)

B is still at the office but is about to leave. The action hasn't started yet, so we use the dictionary form 帰かえる + ところです.

A: "Are? Mada shigoto ga aru no?"
B: "Ie, ima kaeru tokoro desu."

Q3 Write

もしもし、今いま、駅えきに向むかって　ところです。あと5、6分ふんで着つきます。（歩あるく）

Hello? I'm walking toward the station right now. I'll arrive in another 5 or 6 minutes.

Show answerHide answer
Answer歩あるいている（歩あるいているところです）

Grammarている + ところです (in the middle of)

The speaker is walking at this moment and hasn't arrived yet ("5 or 6 more minutes"). An action in progress uses ている + ところ.

Moshimoshi, ima, eki ni mukatte aruite iru tokoro desu. Ato go, roppun de tsukimasu.

Q4 Write

けん「お母かあさん、ばんご飯はん、まだ？」
母はは「今いま　ところよ。もう少すこしで食たべられるよ。」（作つくる）

Ken: "Mom, isn't dinner ready yet?"
Mom: "I'm making it right now. You'll be able to eat soon."

Show answerHide answer
Answer作つくっている（作つくっているところ）

Grammarている + ところ (in the middle of)

Mom is cooking at this moment, and dinner will be ready "in a little while" (もう少すこしで). The action is in progress, so 作つくっている + ところ.

Ken: "Okaasan, bangohan, mada?"
Haha: "Ima tsukutte iru tokoro yo. Mou sukoshi de taberareru yo." The よ endings make it casual family speech.

Q5 Write

コンサートが　ところですね。人ひとがたくさん会場かいじょうから出でてきましたよ。（終おわる）

The concert seems to have just ended. Lots of people have come out of the venue.

Show answerHide answer
Answer終おわった（終おわったところです）

Grammarた form + ところです (just finished)

People are already coming out, so the concert has just ended. Something that has just finished uses the た form + ところ.

Konsaato ga owatta tokoro desu ne. Hito ga takusan kaijou kara dete kimashita yo.

Q6 Write

わたしは日本にほんに　までドイツに住すんでいました。（来くる）

I lived in Germany until I came to Japan.

Show answerHide answer
Answer来くる（来くるまで）

GrammarDictionary form + まで (until)

まで after a verb uses the dictionary form, even when the sentence is about the past. Living in Germany (住すんでいました) is a continuing state that lasted up to the point of coming to Japan, so まで (not までに) fits.

Watashi wa Nihon ni kuru made Doitsu ni sunde imashita.

Instructions

いちばんいいものをえらんでください。

Choose the best option.

Q1 Choose

子こどもは　学校がっこうのことを話はなした。

The child talked about (what happened at) school while ( ).

Show answerHide answer
Answera. なきながら

Grammarます-stem + ながら (two actions at the same time)

ながら needs an action happening alongside the main action. Crying while talking is a natural pair. b 立たつ (stand up) is a one-moment change, not a continuing action, so 立たちながら sounds wrong; you would say 立たって話はなした. c has a different problem: 声こえを出だす is the act of speaking itself, so "spoke while making a loud voice" is redundant; you would say 大おおきい声こえで話はなした.

Kodomo wa nakinagara gakkou no koto o hanashita. "The child cried as they told (us) about school."

Q2 Choose

あのいすに　話はなしましょう。

Let's ( ) on that chair and talk.

Show answerHide answer
Answera. すわって

Grammarて form for a sequence of actions vs. ながら

すわる (to sit down) is a quick, one-time movement, so you first sit, then talk: すわって話はなしましょう. b ながら needs an ongoing action; "while (in the act of) sitting down" doesn't make sense. c ところで is not used like this.

Ano isu ni suwatte hanashimashou.

Q3 Choose

雨あめが　から、タクシーで行いきましょう。

Since it's ( ), let's go by taxi.

Show answerHide answer
Answerb. ふっている

Grammarている for an ongoing state + から (because)

"Because it is raining (now)" uses the ongoing form ふっている + から. a ふって + から would mean "after it rains," which doesn't fit the reason for taking a taxi. c ふったところだ + から is not natural; ところだ is used at the end of a sentence to show a stage of an action, not as a reason.

Ame ga futte iru kara, takushii de ikimashou.

Q4 Choose

このシャツのクリーニング、土曜日どようび　できますか。

Can the dry cleaning of this shirt be done ( ) Saturday?

Show answerHide answer
Answerc. までに

Grammarまでに (by a deadline)

The customer is asking whether the cleaning will be finished by Saturday. "Be finished/done" is a one-time event with a deadline, so までに. b まで would need a continuing action ("keep cleaning until Saturday"). a から ("from Saturday") doesn't fit asking about finishing.

Kono shatsu no kuriiningu, doyoubi made ni dekimasu ka.

Q5 Choose

リーさんはこのニュースをもう　でしょうか。

Does Lee already ( ) this news, I wonder?

Show answerHide answer
Answerb. 知しっている

Grammar知しっている (to know) / misuse of ところ

"To know" is always 知しっている in Japanese. With もう, the question is simply "Does Lee already know?" a and c add ところ, which talks about a stage of an action (about to / in the middle of). Knowing is a state, not an action with stages, so ところ doesn't fit.

Rii-san wa kono nyuusu o mou shitte iru deshou ka.

Q6 Choose

試験しけんが　教室きょうしつに入はいってください。

Please enter the classroom ( ) the exam begins.

Show answerHide answer
Answerc. 始はじまるまでに

GrammarDictionary form + までに (by a deadline)

Entering the classroom is a one-time action that must happen before the deadline (the exam starting), so までに. a ところ can't connect directly to the next verb like this. b 始はじまって would mean "the exam starts, and then enter the room," which is the wrong order.

Shiken ga hajimaru made ni kyoushitsu ni haitte kudasai.
