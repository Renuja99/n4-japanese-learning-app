Grammar points on page 48

1

～そうです

looks like ~ / seems ~ / is about to ~

  • Form: verb ます-stem + そうです (negative: ～そうもありません); い-adj (drop い) / な-adj (drop な) + そうです. Negative for adjectives/nouns: ～くなさそうです / ～ではなさそうです.
  • Exceptions: いい/よい → よさそう; ない/ありません → なさそう.
  • Used to say something is about to happen, judging from what you see (①); to guess a thing's quality or state from how it looks (②③); or to give your judgment or feeling about what will happen (④⑤).
  • Examples from the book:
    1. あ、テーブルの上うえのコップがおちそうですよ。 Oh, the cup on the table looks like it's about to fall!
    2. わあ、おいしそうなケーキですね。 Wow, that cake looks delicious.
    3. ジョンさんはしんぱいそうに電話でんわで話はなしています。 John is talking on the phone, looking worried.
    4. 夏休なつやすみには国くにへ帰かえれそうです。 It looks like I'll be able to go back to my country for the summer holidays.
    5. とてもいい天気てんきです。雨あめはふりそうもありませんね。 The weather's great. It doesn't look like it'll rain at all.
2

～がっています / ～がります

shows signs of feeling ~

  • Form: い-adj (drop い) / な-adj (drop な) + がっています / がります
  • Describes another person's wishes or feelings (usually family members or people below you). For a general tendency rather than the current situation, use ～がります (③).
  • Examples from the book:
    1. 犬いぬが死しにました。母はははさびしがっています。 Our dog died. My mother is feeling lonely.
    2. 子こどもたちはおもしろがってゲームであそんでいます。 The children are having fun playing the game.
    3. 弟おとうとはこわい話はなしをいやがります。 My younger brother hates scary stories.
3

～まま…

leaving as it is / without changing

  • Form: verb た form / ない form; い-adj ～い; な-adj ～な; noun + の + まま
  • A state continues without changing. Often used when the speaker feels that this unchanged state is not good.
  • Examples from the book:
    1. きのう、まどを開あけたままねました。 Yesterday I slept with the window left open.
    2. 手てがきたないままではいけないよ。早はやくあらって。 You can't leave your hands dirty. Wash them quickly.
    3. 今日きょうは4月がつ1日にちですが、カレンダーが先月せんげつのままですよ。 Today is April 1st, but the calendar is still on last month.

れんしゅう1

Practice 18 questions
Instructions

　の中なかの言葉ことばを正ただしい形かたちにして、書かいてください。

Change the word in the brackets ( ) into the correct form and write it in the blank.

Q1 Write

大おおきいじしんがありました。本ほんだなが　そうでした。（たおれる）

There was a big earthquake. The bookshelf looked like it was about to fall over.

Show answerHide answer
Answerたおれ（たおれそうでした）

Grammarます-stem + そうです (about to happen)

たおれます → たおれ + そう.

Ookii jishin ga arimashita. Hondana ga taoresou deshita.

Q2 Write

少すこしつかれました。でも、こんばんはゆっくり　そうです。（ねられる）

I'm a little tired. But it looks like I'll be able to sleep well tonight.

Show answerHide answer
Answerねられ（ねられそうです）

GrammarPotential verb stem + そうです

ねられる is a Group II verb: ねられます → ねられ + そう.

Sukoshi tsukaremashita. Demo, konban wa yukkuri nerare sou desu.

Q3 Write

ほしがたくさん出でているね。あしたは天気てんきが　そうだね。（いい）

There are lots of stars out. It looks like the weather will be good tomorrow.

Show answerHide answer
Answerよさ（よさそう）

GrammarException: いい → よさそう

いい becomes よさそう, not いそう.

Hoshi ga takusan dete iru ne. Ashita wa tenki ga yosasou da ne.

Q4 Write

ちょっと見みましたが、この店みせにはいい品物しなものは　そうですよ。（ない）

I took a quick look, and it seems there aren't any good items in this shop.

Show answerHide answer
Answerなさ（なさそう）

GrammarException: ない → なさそう

ない becomes なさそう.

Chotto mimashita ga, kono mise ni wa ii shinamono wa nasasou desu yo.

Q5 Write

妹いもうとは　がって、おきゃくさんにあいさつしません。（はずかしい）

My younger sister is shy and won't greet the guests.

Show answerHide answer
Answerはずかし（はずかしがって）

Grammarい-adj (drop い) + がる

It's the sister's feeling, so がる. はずかしい → はずかし + がって.

Imouto wa hazukashigatte, okyakusan ni aisatsu shimasen.

Q6 Write

試合しあいにまけました。みんなは　がっています。（ざんねん）

We lost the match. Everyone is disappointed.

Show answerHide answer
Answerざんねん（ざんねんがっています）

Grammarな-adj + がる

な-adjectives attach directly: ざんねん + がっています.

Shiai ni makemashita. Minna wa zannengatte imasu.

Q7 Write

この村むらはいつまでも　ままですね。（むかし）

This village always stays the same as it was in the old days.

Show answerHide answer
Answerむかしの（むかしのまま）

GrammarNoun + の + まま

Nouns need の before まま: むかしのまま.

Kono mura wa itsu made mo mukashi no mama desu ne.

Q8 Write

スリッパを　まま、たたみのへやに入はいらないでください。（はく）

Please don't go into the tatami room with your slippers still on.

Show answerHide answer
Answerはいた（はいたまま）

Grammarた form + まま

"With slippers still on" = はいたまま. Verbs use the た form before まま.

Surippa o haita mama, tatami no heya ni hairanaide kudasai.

れんしゅう2

Practice 213 questions
Instructions

aかbか いいほうをえらんでください。

Choose the better option, a or b.

Q1 Choose

（a わたしは　b あの人ひとは）お金かねがありそうです。

That person looks like they have money.

Show answerHide answer
Answerb. あの人ひとは

Grammarそう is a guess from appearance

そう is for guessing from how things look. You know whether you have money, so you can't guess about yourself. a is wrong.

Ano hito wa okane ga arisou desu.

Q2 Choose

わあ、（a きれいな　b きれいそうな）花はなですね。

Wow, what beautiful flowers.

Show answerHide answer
Answera. きれいな

GrammarDon't use そう for things you can see directly

Beauty is visible right away, so just say きれいな. そう is for qualities you can't directly see (like taste: おいしそう). b sounds like "flowers that seem pretty," which is odd when you're looking at them.

Waa, kirei na hana desu ne.

Q3 Choose

あしたはあまり（a 寒さむくなさそう　b 寒さむそうもない）ですね。

It doesn't look like it'll be very cold tomorrow.

Show answerHide answer
Answera. 寒さむくなさそう

GrammarNegative of adjective + そう: ～くなさそう

For い-adjectives the negative is ～くなさそう. b ～そうもない is the negative pattern for verbs (ふりそうもない), not adjectives.

Ashita wa amari samukunasasou desu ne.

Q4 Choose

わたしはじこの話はなしを聞きいて、（a おどろきました　b おどろきそうでした）。

I was surprised when I heard about the accident.

Show answerHide answer
Answera. おどろきました

GrammarDon't use そう for your own feelings that actually happened

The speaker actually felt surprise, so just say おどろきました. b "I looked like I'd be surprised" doesn't make sense for your own feeling.

Watashi wa jiko no hanashi o kiite, odorokimashita.

Q5 Choose

弟おとうとは（a なきそうな　b なきそうに）顔かおで「ごめんね」と言いいました。

My younger brother said "sorry" with a face that looked like he was about to cry.

Show answerHide answer
Answera. なきそうな

Grammarそうな + noun

そう works like a な-adjective. Before a noun (顔かお) use そうな. b そうに is used before a verb.

Otouto wa nakisou na kao de "gomen ne" to iimashita.

Q6 Choose

子こどもたちは（a 楽たのしそうな　b 楽たのしそうに）歌うたを歌うたっています。

The children are singing songs happily.

Show answerHide answer
Answerb. 楽たのしそうに

Grammarそうに + verb

It describes how they're singing (歌うたっています), so そうに. a 楽たのしそうな歌うた would mean "a song that seems fun," which focuses on the song, not the children.

Kodomotachi wa tanoshisou ni uta o utatte imasu.

Q7 Choose

わたしは来週らいしゅう（a たいいんしそうです　b たいいんできそうです）。

It looks like I'll be able to leave the hospital next week.

Show answerHide answer
Answerb. たいいんできそうです

GrammarPotential + そう for your own situation

When talking about yourself, そう works with a potential verb ("looks like I'll be able to"). a "I look like I'm about to leave hospital" sounds like guessing about your own action, which doesn't fit.

Watashi wa raishuu taiin dekisou desu.

Q8 Choose

ほら、あの子こがおもちゃを（a ほしがっているよ　b ほしがるよ）。

Look, that child wants the toy.

Show answerHide answer
Answera. ほしがっているよ

Grammarがっている (what someone is showing right now)

ほら ("look") points to what's happening now: ほしがっている. b ほしがる is for a general habit.

Hora, ano ko ga omocha o hoshigatte iru yo.

Q9 Choose

ちゅうしゃはいたいです。（a わたしは　b 子こどもたちは）とてもいやがります。

Injections hurt. Children really hate them.

Show answerHide answer
Answerb. 子こどもたちは

Grammarがる is for other people's feelings

いやがる describes others' feelings, and the general habit form がります fits "children (in general)." a For yourself you'd say いやです.

Chuusha wa itai desu. Kodomotachi wa totemo iyagarimasu.

Q10 Choose

あの人ひとは（a まじめそうですね　b まじめがっていますね）。

That person looks serious (diligent), doesn't he/she?

Show answerHide answer
Answera. まじめそうですね

Grammarそう (appearance) vs. がる (feelings)

まじめ is a personality trait, not a feeling, so がる can't be used. You can guess it from appearance: まじめそう.

Ano hito wa majimesou desu ne.

Q11 Choose

バスに（a 乗のって　b 乗のったまま）仕事しごとへ行いきます。

I take the bus to work.

Show answerHide answer
Answera. 乗のって

Grammarて form for means / まま is for unchanged (often undesirable) states

"Take the bus and go to work" is just 乗のって行いきます. b まま means "while still in a state that should have changed," which is strange here (you're supposed to stay on the bus).

Basu ni notte shigoto e ikimasu.

Q12 Choose

めがねをかけたまま（a 本ほんを読よみました　b ねました）。

I fell asleep with my glasses still on.

Show answerHide answer
Answerb. ねました

Grammarまま (state that should have changed but didn't)

You normally take off glasses before sleeping, so "slept with them still on" fits まま. a Reading with glasses on is normal, so まま sounds odd.

Megane o kaketa mama nemashita.

Q13 Choose

母ははは朝あさ出でかけたまま（a まだ家いえに帰かえりません　b すぐ家いえに帰かえりました）。

My mother went out in the morning and still hasn't come home.

Show answerHide answer
Answera. まだ家いえに帰かえりません

Grammarまま (the state continues)

まま means the "went out" state is still continuing, so she hasn't come back. b "came back right away" contradicts まま.

Haha wa asa dekaketa mama mada ie ni kaerimasen.
